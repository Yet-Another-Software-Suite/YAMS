Detailed Description

Active disturbance rejection controller.

Written by FRC Team 8331, who contributed active disturbance rejection control to YAMS.

An active disturbance rejection controller treats every unmodeled effect (friction, gravity, back-EMF, coupling, dropped battery voltage, external loads) as a single "total disturbance" and estimates it online with an extended state observer. The controller then cancels the estimate while driving the modeled part of the plant with a bandwidth-parameterized control law, so it needs very little model: just the system order and an approximate input gain b0.

Most teams do not construct this class directly; they describe the controller with ADRCConfig and the motor controller builds and runs it.

Plant orders 1 through 4 are supported. The extended state observer is one order higher than the plant, with its final state holding the disturbance estimate. Gains come from a single closed-loop bandwidth wc; the observer bandwidth is 4 * wc.

The controller keeps only its running state: the observer's estimate, its last outputs and the tracking differentiator. Everything else it reads from its ADRCConfig every step, including the observer and control gains, which the config works out again whenever its settling time, bandwidth scale or b0 changes. The system order, input delay and tracking differentiator are fixed when the controller is initialized.

The controller does not limit or ramp its output. A motor controller running it limits and ramps the output itself, with WithClosedLoopMaxVoltage and WithClosedLoopRampRate, and tells the observer the voltage it applied. Run on its own, clamp the output to what the actuator can apply, ramp it with WPILib's SlewRateLimiter if it needs one, and tell the observer what was applied with Calculate(measurement, setpoint, applied), so a clamped output is not mistaken for a disturbance.

auto config = std::make_shared<yams::math::ADRCConfig>();
config->WithSystemOrder(yams::math::ADRCConfig::SystemOrder::FIRST).WithSettlingTime(200_ms);
yams::math::ADRCController adrc{config};
// b0, which the config leaves unset, and the control period.
adrc.Initialize(b0, 20_ms);
// Every loop: clamp the output, and tell the observer what was applied last loop.
wpi::units::volt_t volts = std::clamp(adrc.Calculate(measurement, setpoint, appliedVoltage), -12_V, 12_V);

Run one control step on a moving setpoint, such as a motion profile's, telling the observer what the actuator applied since the last step.

The controller is given the setpoint where it is now, at the start of the control period, and where it will be at the end, as WPILib's feedforwards are. It follows the setpoint now, and feeds forward how it moves over the period: a first order loop the average rate over the period, and a higher order loop the rate now and the change of rate over the period. Given only the setpoint at the end of the period, its stiff feedback would pull the mechanism a period ahead of the profile. A setpoint standing still is Reference::Still(setpoint) for both. A tracking differentiator, when the config turns one on, replaces the rates with its own.
